Most private companies in Abuja never choose their IT support. They inherit it.

It starts as a phone number. Someone's cousin, or the engineer who set up the
first router. Five years later the company has 60 staff across two offices, one
in Wuse and one in Gwarinpa, and support is still that phone number. A
lot of IT support for companies in Abuja works this way. Nobody decided it.

Whether to hire your own IT staff is a separate question, and we have covered
it in [in-house vs outsourced IT support](/insights/in-house-vs-outsourced-it-support-nigeria).
This piece assumes you are buying support from outside, and asks the next
question: which kind?

## What support should cover at 10 to 150 staff

Before comparing models, write down what you need covered. At this size the
list is longer than most owners expect:

- **The desks.** Laptops, desktops, monitors and the printers people fight over.
- **The network.** Router, switches, Wi-Fi, and the internet links, including
  the conversation with your ISP when a link goes down.
- **Accounts and email.** Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace, and who
  holds the administrator password for the domain.
- **Joiners and leavers.** A new hire with a working laptop on day one. A
  leaver locked out of everything on their last day.
- **Backup.** Somebody checking that it ran, and occasionally restoring a file
  to prove it works.
- **Power on the IT side.** The UPS units on servers and critical desks, and
  their batteries, which wear out on a cycle.
- **Warranty claims and suppliers.** Logging a fault with the manufacturer,
  chasing a replacement, keeping the paperwork.
- **Records.** What you own, where it sits, who uses it, and when it comes off
  warranty.

The two items most often missing are leavers and records. A company that
cannot say who holds the domain password has a support gap, whatever it is
paying.

## The three models, as they actually work

### Ad-hoc call-out

You call when something breaks. An engineer comes out and invoices per visit or
per hour.

For a 10-person office with everything in the cloud, this can be the right
answer. There is no monthly cost and no contract. The cost is everything the
model does not do. Every visit starts from zero, because nobody holds a record
of your estate. Nobody does preventive work, because nobody is paid to. And
there is no response commitment. When the engineer has three callers on the
same morning, you are in the queue with everyone else.

### Retained support

You pay a fixed monthly fee for a defined amount of support: a set number of
on-site hours or visits, plus remote help by phone or remote session. Usually
the same engineer, or a small team, who learns your setup.

The gain is memory and priority. The engineer knows the printer on the second
floor needs its driver reinstalled after every Windows update. Response targets
can be written down. What retained support often leaves out is the work nobody
asks for: patching and backup checks. Read the scope. If it
says "support as required", it is call-out with a monthly invoice.

### Fully managed

The provider takes responsibility for outcomes, not just visits. That means
monitoring of servers and internet links, patching on a schedule,
backup checks, a helpdesk, on-site visits, and response targets in a written
SLA with reporting against them. Pricing is usually per user or per device per
month. This is what we mean by [managed services](/services/managed-services).

The gain is that problems get found before staff report them. A disk throwing
errors gets replaced on a Tuesday afternoon instead of failing on a Monday
morning. The cost is a higher monthly figure and an onboarding period where the
provider documents and cleans up what you already have.

## What decides the price

We do not publish a naira figure for any of the three, because the honest
answer depends on variables only your company can supply. These are the ones
that move it:

1. **Users and devices.** The count of people and the count of things they
   use. A company with 40 staff and 40 laptops prices differently from one
   with 40 staff, 25 desktops, 6 shared machines and a server.
2. **Sites.** One office is one engineer trip. Two offices across the FCT,
   say Central Business District and Lugbe, means travel time on every on-site
   visit, and someone has to pay for it.
3. **Hours of cover.** Monday to Friday, 8am to 5pm, is the base. Saturday
   cover, or cover for a team that works late before bid deadlines, adds cost.
4. **Response targets.** A four-hour on-site target costs more than next
   business day. Ask yourself what a lost afternoon actually costs before you
   pay for the faster number.
5. **The state of the estate.** Twenty identical laptops bought together are
   cheap to support. Twenty machines bought from eight places over six years,
   with no records and three Windows versions, are not. The first month of any
   retained or managed contract is mostly paying for this.
6. **What is in scope.** Hardware purchases and project work, such as a new
   office fit-out, are usually billed separately. Confirm in writing.

## How to tell which one you need

Answer four questions honestly.

**How much does an outage cost you?** If the internet drops for half a day,
does the company lose half a day of revenue, or does everyone switch to their
phone hotspot and carry on? The first company needs monitoring. The second may
not.

**How many calls did you make last quarter?** Count them. If the answer is two,
call-out is fine. If it is twenty, you are already paying for retained support,
just at a worse rate and with no memory.

**Do you hold data you are obliged to protect?** A law firm, a clinic, an
accounting practice and an HR consultancy all hold personal data under the
Nigeria Data Protection Act. Somebody has to own patching and access control.
Call-out support does not own anything.

**Could you survive your engineer leaving?** If one person holds every
password, the risk is the same whether that person is staff or a freelancer.

Now the honest part. A company of 12 people, all in Microsoft 365, one office,
no server, does not need fully managed support. Retained support with a small
number of on-site hours is enough, and we will say so. Fully managed starts to
earn its cost somewhere past 30 or 40 users. Earlier if downtime has a direct
price or you hold sensitive data.

One more thing. Do not buy fully managed support to fix a mess. If the estate
is chaotic, treat the clean-up as a project with a fixed scope and a fixed
price. Then put it under a support contract. Folding the clean-up into a
monthly fee hides its cost and makes the contract impossible to judge.

## IT support for companies in Abuja: what being local changes

Remote support works from anywhere. On-site support does not. A dead switch or
a laptop that will not boot needs a person in the room, and
in Abuja the variable that decides how fast that happens is the distance from
where the engineer starts the day.

Ask any provider where their engineers are based. A Lagos firm with "Abuja
coverage" may mean a flight or a subcontractor. An Abuja firm may still be an
hour from your site in morning traffic if you are in Kubwa and they are in
Garki. Neither is wrong. You just need to know before you sign a response
target.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does IT support cost for a company in Abuja?

Five things set it: user and device count, number of sites, hours of cover,
response targets and the state of what you already own. A single figure would mislead
you. A provider who has seen your estate can give you a line-itemised monthly
number. One who has not is guessing.

### Is call-out IT support ever the right choice?

Yes. For a small office with fewer than about 15 staff, cloud email and files,
no server and a low cost of downtime, call-out support is often the cheapest
sensible option. It stops being sensible once you are calling more than two or
three times a month.

### What is the difference between retained and managed IT support?

Retained support buys a set amount of an engineer's time. Managed support buys
an outcome: monitored systems, scheduled patching, checked backups and response
targets in a written SLA. Retained reacts faster than call-out. Managed is the
only one of the three that looks for problems before you report them.

### Should our support provider also supply our hardware?

Often it helps, because the people who support the machines then hold the
purchase records and warranty paperwork. It is not required. If you keep them
separate, make sure the asset register and warranty documents sit with you, not
with either supplier.
