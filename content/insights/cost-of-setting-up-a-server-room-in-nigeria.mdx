Two questions decide it, and neither is about servers. How will you power the
room when the grid is not there, and how will you keep it cool?

The cost of setting up a server room in Nigeria is not one number. It is the sum
of several decisions, and the two above move it most. They are also the two the
equipment quotation tends to hide.

We do not publish a naira figure for "a server room" because it would be wrong
in two directions at once: wrong for your requirement, and stale within weeks,
given how the naira has moved against the dollar through 2026. What holds steady
is the structure of the cost. Understand that and any quote you receive becomes
something you can interrogate rather than something you have to trust.

## Power and cooling, not the servers

Most people costing a server room start with the servers, storage and network
switches. That equipment matters. In a Nigerian build it is frequently not the
largest share of the project. The room around the rack routinely rivals or
exceeds the cost of the hardware inside it, and budgets that assume the opposite
are the ones that blow up halfway through.

Powering the room means paying for several things at once:

- **UPS and batteries**, sized to carry the full IT load long enough to ride
  through the switchover to standby power, and to shut systems down cleanly if
  the outage outlasts the batteries. Runtime is a design choice with a direct
  cost. More minutes means more battery.
- **Standby generation and an automatic transfer switch**, because in most of
  Nigeria the UPS is a bridge to the generator, not the primary backup. The
  generator has to be sized for the room's real draw plus cooling, not just the
  servers.
- **Power conditioning**, to protect equipment from the surges and sags of
  mains-to-generator switchover. These are the dirty-power events that quietly
  kill power supplies and drives.

Two things push this line up sharply: the redundancy you choose, where a second
UPS or generator roughly doubles parts of it, and the runtime you specify. It is
entirely normal for power provisioning to be the single largest capital item in
a Nigerian server room.

Cooling sits on top of that. Servers reject heat continuously and Nigerian
ambient temperatures give a cooling system no help. The choice is a real
engineering one: comfort-style air conditioning, cheaper to buy but less
reliable for a room that runs around the clock, against precision cooling
designed for continuous IT load and redundancy. Cooling then feeds back into the
power line, because it is part of the load the UPS and generator must carry.
That is why under-costed cooling shows up twice.

## The hardware is priced in dollars

Servers, storage and networking are specified to the workload, and requirements
here legitimately vary by an order of magnitude. A single-server room for a
branch office is a different animal from a small on-premise data room for an
enterprise.

The important local point is not the unit price. It is the currency. Enterprise
IT hardware is imported and **invoiced in US dollars**, then converted to naira
at the prevailing rate. The naira price of the same equipment, at the same list
price, tracks the exchange rate on the day the order is placed. As at Q3 2026,
with the naira volatile against the dollar, a hardware budget fixed months
earlier can be materially wrong by the time it is committed, through no change
in specification.

Any figure in this category has to be treated as valid on a stated date and
rechecked at order time. That is a fact about the currency, not a hedge.

## The unglamorous lines, and the lead time

The boring items are real budget lines: proper
[structured cabling to a recognised standard](/insights/structured-cabling-standards-for-nigerian-offices),
racks and cable management, fire detection and ideally suppression appropriate
to the room's value, physical access control, and environmental monitoring so
you learn about a cooling or power problem before the hardware does. None is the
largest line. Omitting them is how a cheaper quote is made to look competitive
while quietly moving risk onto you.

Lead time belongs in the same section because it is a cost, not just a date.
The hardware is imported, so import and clearing time is part of the real cost
of the project. A decision made late forces either an expensive expedite or a
delay to the whole build, and holding spares locally against a future failure
carries its own cost that a resilient design should account for. This is why a
server-room decision usually has to be taken a quarter or more before the room
is needed.

## What it costs after go-live

Setup is capital. Running the room is operating cost, and it belongs in the same
business case. Generator fuel and maintenance, UPS battery replacement on a
cycle, cooling servicing and support all recur. A server room costed only to the
point of switch-on has budgeted for half the decision.

## Building a server room cost you can trust

Build the budget the way the room is built: from the load outward. Establish the
IT load and the redundancy you actually need. Size power and cooling to that
load. Add the fabric and the lead-time reality. Separate one-time capital from
ongoing run cost, and treat the FX-exposed lines as valid on a date and
rechecked at order.

One thing worth saying plainly, because it saves money: you can usually phase
this. Build the power and cooling for the intended final load, then add IT
hardware as you need it. What you should not do is undersize the power and
cooling foundation, because retrofitting redundancy into a live room is
expensive and disruptive in a way that adding a server is not.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does it cost to set up a server room in Nigeria?

There is no single credible figure. The cost is driven by your IT load, the
redundancy you choose, and above all how you provision power and cooling, with
the hardware priced in US dollars and converted at the prevailing rate. Rather
than quote a naira number that would be wrong for your requirement and stale
within weeks, we scope the room from its load outward and cost each driver
against your specification, dated and rechecked at order time.

### Why is power such a large part of the cost?

Because in most of Nigeria the room cannot assume the grid. You are paying for
UPS and batteries to ride through outages, standby generation and an ATS to
carry the load when the grid is down, and power conditioning to protect
equipment on switchover. Redundancy and runtime choices can make power
provisioning the single largest capital line in the build, often more than the
servers themselves.

### Why won't you give a fixed naira price up front?

Because enterprise IT hardware is imported and invoiced in US dollars, so its
naira price tracks the exchange rate on the day of purchase. As at Q3 2026 that
rate has been volatile, which means a fixed figure quoted in advance can mislead
through currency movement alone. We quote against a dated specification and
recheck the FX-exposed lines at order. A stale or invented number in a cost
estimate destroys the trust the estimate exists to build.

### Can we build the room in stages to spread the cost?

Often, yes, and for most organisations it is the right call. The part it is
dangerous to undersize is the power and cooling foundation, because retrofitting
redundancy into a live room is expensive and disruptive. Build power and cooling
for the intended final load, then phase the IT hardware, so the room can grow
without being rebuilt.
